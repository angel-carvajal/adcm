# Commercial surface patterns — STEP 3b

Runs only when Q9 says the business sells something.

**Why it is a separate search:** the site with the best vibe is rarely the best
at selling. Scoring one reference for both guarantees a strong hero above a
generic product section. Search these independently, score them independently,
and let a different reference win here.

Collect at least **8 candidates**, independent of the 15 from STEP 3.

---

## A. How the offering is shown

| Pattern | Fits | Signals it is right |
|---|---|---|
| **Filterable catalog** | many SKUs, comparable specs | >12 items, buyers narrow by attribute |
| **Service cards** | 3–6 services, no unit price | intangible work, "what we do" |
| **Menu list** | food, typographic, price-per-line | many cheap items, no images needed |
| **Comparison table** | tiers where the choice is the point | buyer is deciding *which*, not *whether* |
| **Package cards** | 3–5 bundles at fixed prices | productized services |
| **Live-price configurator** | build-to-order, options change price | trailers, vehicles, furniture, machinery |
| **Spec sheet** | technical buyers, one hero product | dimensions and materials drive the decision |
| **Immersive carousel** | few premium products, story matters | luxury, one product per screen |
| **Work gallery** | the past work *is* the product | contractors, studios, photographers |
| **Editorial product story** | one flagship, high consideration | long scroll, narrative before price |

## B. How it converts

| Pattern | Friction | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Public price + cart | lowest | standardized goods, low ticket |
| "From $X" + contact | low | variable scope, anchors expectation |
| Multi-step quote wizard | medium | configurable high-ticket; qualifies the lead |
| Single quote form | medium | few variables, fast reply promised |
| Book a call | medium | consultative sales, high ticket |
| WhatsApp / DM | lowest | LATAM, mobile-first, informal trust |
| Request a catalog | low | long cycle; a lead magnet, not a sale |
| Pricing table + toggle | low | subscription with billing periods |

**Quote-only is a decision, not a default.** If the business hides price, the
page has to pay for that friction with an anchor ("from $X", a past-project
range, or a financing figure). Score a quote-only reference that shows no price
signal at all as weak.

## C. What to evaluate in every candidate

Walk the live demo — do not judge from a thumbnail.

1. **Price depth.** How many scrolls from the top does a number first appear?
2. **Clicks to convert.** From landing to submitted intent.
3. **CTA persistence.** Does it stick, or must the user scroll back?
4. **Proof adjacency.** Is social proof within one viewport of the price?
5. **Objection handling.** FAQ, warranty, financing, lead time, returns — where?
6. **Form friction.** Field count, required fields, whether price appears before the form.
7. **Mobile order.** On mobile, does price or CTA come before the long story?
8. **Specificity.** Does the page name real numbers, or hide behind adjectives?

Record these as a short table per candidate. They become `.design/commerce.md`.

## D. Reuse the project's real quoting logic

If a quote-generating skill exists for this business
(e.g. `<business>-quote-generator` or an equivalent), **read it before designing
the flow**. The designed wizard must
collect exactly what the real quoting logic needs — no more, no fewer steps.
A beautiful flow that asks the wrong questions is worse than an ugly one.
