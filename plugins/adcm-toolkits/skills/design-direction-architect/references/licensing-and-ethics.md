# Licensing and ethics

Read before writing anything into `.design/`.

## The rule that governs all of them

**Reference ≠ copy.** What may be taken from a reference: structure, proportion,
rhythm, type *scale* (not the licensed typeface file), palette *strategy*, motion
*pattern*, information hierarchy. What may never be taken: copy text, images,
illustrations, icons, video, logos, or a section reproduced closely enough that a
reader would recognize the original.

If the honest description of what you did is "I rebuilt their page with
different words", stop.

## Per source

**Framer marketplace.** Templates are commercial works sold under the author's
license. Walking a public demo to study it is fine. Downloading, remixing or
reproducing a template without buying it is not. Free templates are still
licensed — free is a price, not a waiver. Record the author's name alongside any
reference that materially shaped the direction.

*Where the line sits when you run the extractor.* Reading computed styles off a
public page and learning the **relationships** — ground-to-accent distance,
display-to-body ratio, mono tracking, section height rhythm — is design research,
and those ratios are ideas, not expression. Lifting the template's specific
palette, its layout, its section copy or its assets into the project is
reproduction. The test: could the author look at the result and recognize their
page? If yes, you copied. Never buy a template in order to study it, and never
present an extraction as if the project had earned that design.

**Pinterest.** Pins are third-party content, usually reposted without the
original creator's involvement. Treat a pin as a *pointer*, never as an asset.
Never copy an image out of Pinterest into a project. When a pin links to a live
site, credit and study the live site, not the pin. Do not screenshot a
logged-in Pinterest view into `.design/refs/`.

**21st.dev.** Components carry the publishing author's license and are installed
through the official command. Never transcribe component code by hand from a
preview. Never call `get_component` during research — that is a paid retrieval
and research is metadata-only.

**Pexels.** Free for commercial use; attribution appreciated, not required.
Prohibited: reselling unaltered copies, implying that an identifiable person or
brand in the image endorses the product, and identifiable-person usage that is
defamatory or sensitive. Record every asset's source URL in `.design/assets.md`
so provenance outlives the session.

## Trademarks and lookalikes

Studying a competitor's site is normal practice. Producing something a customer
could mistake for that competitor is not. If the direction converges on a
recognizable brand's identity — their exact palette plus their typeface plus
their layout — say so and change one of the three.

## Honesty in the pack

- Never write a URL you did not open.
- Never present a Dribbble concept as a shipped site.
- Never claim coverage from a source that was unavailable. Name the gap.
- Never let stock imagery stand in for the product being sold without the
  `REAL-PHOTO-NEEDED` marker.
